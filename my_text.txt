What's up guys — NVSU immersion vlog. Three days, five destinations, all over Laguna and Metro Manila. I'm Dann, a 3rd year Computer Science Robotics Student. Let's get into it.

We left at 4 AM from Bayombong campus. Cold, dark, most people half asleep on the bus. First stop was Aritao, Nueva Vizcaya. Quick rest, then San Jose. But then further along, one of the IT buses broke down in Nueva Ecija. Thirty minutes just sitting on the side of the road. Eventually got moving again. At 9:15 we hit City Mall SCTEX for food. Then at 10:40 we transferred to a proper tourist bus in Pandi, Bulacan — that's where we met Ma'am Morena, our tour guide for the whole trip. We ate lunch on the bus while she pointed out landmarks going into NCR — schools, media stations, buildings. And then the skyway. Looking out at that skyline for the first time like that — it was different. Really different.

First stop — DOST in Taguig City. The Philippine Science Heritage Center. They showed us a documentary about 44 Filipino National Scientists. I didn't know we had that many. Gregorio Y. Zara. Fe Del Mundo. Lourdes J. Cruz. People who actually built science in this country. Then came the hologram avatars — you tap the screen, pick a question, and the hologram answers you. I kept thinking about the programming behind that. How does it decide which response to play? Then there was this room designed like a spaceship, showing Earth from space, real-time climate change visuals. Not a lecture — an actual experience. After that, STII — the Science and Technology Information Institute. They walked us through their academic database for finding Filipino research papers. Sounds simple but it's going to be useful for thesis.

Back to Bulacan. Amana Waterpark — rooms booked for four people. Seven of us ended up in one room. I still don't know how that happened. But we had dinner, then headed to the wave pool at 8:30. The park had everything — Avengers theme, Shrek, dinosaurs, all of it. Swam for over an hour. After a full day of travel and DOST, nobody even cared about the room anymore. That was night one.

Day two. Bocaue, Bulacan. Dr. Yanga's Colleges Inc. And right outside — this giant robot called Metanoia. Bigger than I expected. Inside, two areas. First was the Bocaue Institute with their history room — spinning LED display, holograms, the whole setup. Then the ALAB lab — Achievement and Innovation Lab. Student-built robots everywhere. One could speak Korean, Japanese, and Filipino. Running on language models they programmed themselves. But the one that stuck with me was the aquatic cleaning robot — designed to remove moss and debris from Philippine waterways. It's not flashy, but it's solving a real local problem. And the walls had trophies from international robotics competitions. Students here competing globally with machines they built.

Next was Hytec Power Inc. in Quezon City. Training facility — drones with protective frames, Vex Robotics kits, EV prototypes, all in one building. But the tractor simulator got me. Software that replicates the exact physical feel of driving a real tractor — the vibration, the controls, everything — so people can train on heavy machinery safely without actual risk. That's programming with real stakes. Some NVSU students do their OJT here too, which I didn't know.

Tiara Oriental Hotel in Makati. Four per room this time — huge upgrade from Amana. We had dinner, swam in the indoor pool, even hit the gym. Night two.

Day three. Lucky Me Noodle Factory in Santa Rosa, Laguna. No photos allowed inside — factory rules. But those machines were running at a pace that looked impossible. Thousands of noodle packs per minute, everything timed perfectly. We actually danced on the factory floor before leaving. Got a free pack each on the way out.

Free time at Greenfield City. Saw BonChon, saw the prices, kept walking. Ended up at Little Don Juan. Made it back to the bus just in time.

Then Western Digital in Biñan, Laguna. Best stop of the entire trip. And it wasn't close. No photos allowed inside — you're just going to have to trust me on this. The factory was the most organized place I have ever been. Their server room — every cable perfectly routed, color-coded, nothing out of place. Cable management at a level I didn't think was possible in a working facility.

They walked us through their production process: starts with a wafer, gets cut into quads, quads become rowbars, rowbars become sliders, and sliders are assembled into the Head Gimbal Assembly — the HGA. That's the component inside every hard drive that reads and writes all your data. We held an actual wafer. The precision is insane.

Then the AI seminar. They showed three systems they run inside the factory. Web Scraper AI that automatically gathers data from the internet. A Knowledge-Based AI Assistant built for their engineers to solve technical problems faster. And then the Autonomous Workflow AI Agent — one system connecting every single department. Welding, software, programming, quality control — all integrated, all communicating, all running together in real time. I was already into AI before this trip. But seeing it operate an entire factory at scale, in real time, with actual production on the line — that locked in exactly what I want to be working on in the future.

Last stop — Mall of Asia. We got there right at sunset over Manila Bay. Sky turning orange, place packed, just walked around with no agenda for once. Had Jollibee, took photos with friends. Most relaxed we'd been the whole trip. By 10 PM we transferred back to the original NVSU bus and started the overnight ride home. Slept the whole way.

Five stops. Three days. I'm Dann — BSCS Robotics, NVSU-CITE 3-B. That was our immersion.
